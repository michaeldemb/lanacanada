Express Entry After the Reset: New Draw Results and What 2027 May Bring

Express Entry November 2026
← Back to Blog

For almost four weeks this fall, Express Entry went quiet: no draws and no invitations, while IRCC re-scored every profile in the pool under new rules. Draws have now resumed, and we have real numbers to look at. Here is what changed, what the first draws show and what 2027 may look like.

The CRS change in brief

Since November 3, your CRS score can get a boost if your occupation is on IRCC's high-wage list:

  • 12+ months of Canadian work in a high-wage occupation: up to +70 points.
  • A job offer in a high-wage occupation: up to +100 points, and no LMIA is needed.

You get whichever is higher, not both. The part most people miss: "high-wage" is about your occupation, not your paycheque. IRCC looks at the national median wage for your NOC code. A junior developer earning $60K can qualify, while a well-paid worker in an occupation not on the list gets no extra points. See our CRS score guide for the basics.

The first three draws after the reset

  • Nov 23, Provincial Nominees: 1,999 invitations, cut-off 673. That is more than double October's PNP draw, and the cut-off dropped from 761. Nominees accumulated during the pause and IRCC cleared a large batch at once.
  • Nov 25, French speakers: 6,000 invitations, cut-off 401. That is 1,000 more invitations than October's French draw, with a cut-off 6 points lower.
  • Nov 26, STEM: 600 invitations, cut-off 547. This is the first STEM draw since 2024. The last few STEM draws closed around 481-491. Most STEM jobs are on the high-wage list, so almost everyone in the category received a boost at the same time. A small draw plus boosted scores means a very high bar.

What may come next for draws

These are educated guesses, not promises.

  • CEC cut-offs will likely climb. The last Canadian Experience Class draw (Oct 15) closed at 519. Many CEC candidates work in high-wage jobs and gained 20-70 points. We expect the first CEC draw under the new rules to land somewhere in the 530s-550s.
  • French stays the most accessible route. We expect cut-offs to stay roughly in the 390-420 range.
  • More PNP draws before the holidays. Another is widely expected in early December.
  • The hidden squeeze. If your NOC is not on the list, your score did not change, but people around you may have jumped ahead. Your number is the same; your rank may have slipped.

What 2027 looks like

On November 5, Canada released its new Immigration Levels Plan. The headline number stays at 380,000 new permanent residents a year, but the mix is shifting:

  • Express Entry: 100,000 spots (down from 111,000).
  • Provincial Nominee Programs: 95,000 spots (up from 92,500).
  • French speakers outside Quebec: 9.5% of newcomers in 2027, rising to 12% by 2029.
  • New international students: 150,000 (down from 155,000).
  • New temporary workers: 220,000 (down from 230,000).
  • A one-time measure for up to 200,000 people already living in Canada, on top of the 380,000.

About that 200,000 measure: there is no application, form or portal yet. If anyone offers to sell you a spot, walk away.

Our six predictions for 2027

  • Provinces become the main highway. Fewer federal spots and more provincial ones make a nomination worth more. See Provincial Nominee Programs.
  • Being in Canada is the biggest advantage. Between the 200,000 measure and the new Canadian experience points, people already working here have the inside lane.
  • French keeps paying off. French targets rise every year; if you have been thinking about learning French, 2027 is a good year to start.
  • Express Entry gets more competitive. Fewer spots and boosted scores mean we do not expect general cut-offs to return to early-2026 levels soon.
  • Fewer, more focused categories. IRCC has said it is considering narrowing the number or eligibility of category draws for 2027.
  • The next big reform gets closer. IRCC has proposed replacing FSW, CEC and FST with one "Federal High-Skilled" program. It is still a proposal, but 2027 could bring real details.

What to do this week

  • Look up your NOC code on IRCC's high-wage occupations list.
  • Log in and check your recalculated CRS score; do not rely on an old screenshot.
  • No high-wage points and under 500? Look seriously at French, a provincial nomination or a category draw that fits you.
  • Have a job offer? Check whether it now qualifies, even without an LMIA.
  • Already in Canada on a work permit? Keep your status valid and your documents ready for 2027. Our page on Express Entry explains the process.
