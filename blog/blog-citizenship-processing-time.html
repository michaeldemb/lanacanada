Canadian Citizenship Processing Time: What Happens at Each Stage

Citizenship August 2026
← Back to Blog

IRCC measures citizenship processing time from the day it receives a complete application to the day you take the Oath of Citizenship. The published figure is an average, it changes regularly, and it says nothing about what actually happens to your file in between. This article walks through each stage, explains what makes individual applications faster or slower than the average, and tells you what you can do at each point.

Looking for the current processing time? IRCC publishes the up-to-date figure in its official processing times tool. After you apply, follow your own file in the citizenship application status tracker.

The Stages of a Citizenship Application

1. Intake and Completeness Check

When your application arrives, IRCC first checks that it is complete: every form signed, every required document included, the fee paid, and the physical presence calculation attached. Incomplete applications are not queued for processing — they are returned to you, and you lose your place entirely. This is the single most avoidable delay in the whole process.

2. Acknowledgement of Receipt (AOR)

Once the completeness check passes, IRCC issues an Acknowledgement of Receipt — your confirmation that the application has formally entered processing. The gap between mailing or submitting your application and receiving the AOR reflects IRCC's intake queue and varies with volume; it is normal for this step to take time, and the wait before AOR does not mean something is wrong. After AOR, you can register for the online status tracker and see your file's progress through each subsequent stage.

3. Background Verification and Presence Review

IRCC verifies your immigration history, runs criminal and security checks, and reviews your declared physical presence against government records, including travel history. Most applications pass through this stage without you hearing anything — silence here is normal. If the reviewing officer finds gaps or inconsistencies, this is where a Residence Questionnaire (RQ) or a request for additional documents is issued.

4. The Citizenship Test and Interview

Applicants aged 18–54 receive a test invitation: 20 questions, 30 minutes, 15 correct answers to pass, based on the official Discover Canada study guide. Around the test, an official may also verify your original documents and assess your English or French. Missing a test invitation without rescheduling is a common self-inflicted delay — IRCC communicates by email, so watch your inbox (and spam folder) and keep your contact details current.

5. Decision

After the test and any follow-up review, a citizenship officer makes the final decision. If you did not pass the test on the first attempt, you are invited to a second; after a second unsuccessful attempt, you attend a hearing with an official rather than being refused automatically.

6. The Oath of Citizenship

Approved applicants are invited to a citizenship ceremony. You become a Canadian citizen at the moment you take the oath — and the processing clock, as IRCC measures it, stops here. Your citizenship certificate is issued as proof afterwards.

Why Some Applications Are Faster Than the Average

The published processing time is an average across every kind of file. Applications that consistently beat it share the same traits: a physical presence calculation with a comfortable buffer above the 1,095-day minimum, a travel history that matches border records exactly, clear language evidence, prompt responses to every IRCC request, and contact details that never go stale. None of this is luck — it is all within your control before you apply. Our guide to the citizenship grant covers the eligibility requirements in detail.

What Slows an Application Down

  • A Residence Questionnaire (RQ). If your declared presence raises questions — long absences, gaps in the record, or discrepancies with border data — IRCC may issue an RQ requesting detailed proof of your time in Canada. Answering it thoroughly matters more than answering it quickly.
  • Travel history discrepancies. Even honest mistakes — a forgotten weekend trip across the border — can push a file into deeper review. This is the most common trigger.
  • Background check complications. Criminal charges, unresolved proceedings, or name matches requiring clarification extend the security stage.
  • Missed communications. A test invitation or document request that goes unanswered stalls the file until it is resolved — or leads to the application being closed as abandoned.
  • Requests for additional documents. Each round trip adds time; each incomplete response adds another.

When to Worry — and What You Can Do

Compare your file's age against the current published average, not against other applicants' anecdotes. If you are within the published time, the honest advice is patience: status-check requests do not speed up processing.

If your application has meaningfully exceeded the published time, you have escalating options: check the status tracker for the stage your file is actually at, submit an IRCC webform inquiry, request your file's internal notes (GCMS notes) to see what is happening behind the scenes, or contact your Member of Parliament's constituency office, which can make status inquiries on your behalf. In cases of extreme, unexplained delay, a Federal Court application called a writ of mandamus can compel IRCC to make a decision — a step worth discussing with a licensed professional before pursuing.

An RCIC can review a delayed file, identify which stage it is stuck at and why, and prepare a response that resolves the issue rather than prolonging it.
